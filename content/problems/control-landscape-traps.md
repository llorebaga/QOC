## Statement

Consider a closed $N$-level system driven by one real control $f$,

$$i\,\dot U_t = \big(H_0 + f(t)\,V\big)\,U_t, \qquad U_0 = I, \qquad f \in L^2([0,T];\mathbb R),$$

with $H_0$ and $V$ Hermitian $N\times N$ matrices, and assume that it is completely
[[controllability|controllable]] (the Lie algebra generated by $iH_0$ and $iV$ is $\mathfrak{su}(N)$ or
$\mathfrak u(N)$). Typical objectives are

- an expectation value, $J_O(f) = \mathrm{Tr}\big[O\,U_T\,\rho_0\,U_T^\dagger\big]$, for an observable $O$ and an
  initial state $\rho_0$;
- a transition probability, $J_{i\to f}(f) = |\langle f|U_T|i\rangle|^2$;
- a gate fidelity, $J_W(f) = \tfrac{1}{N^2}\big|\mathrm{Tr}\big(W^\dagger U_T\big)\big|^2$, for a target gate $W$.

A control $f$ is a **trap** if it is a local maximum of $J$ but $J(f) < \sup J$ [@volkov2025stronger].

**Question.** Is there, for every controllable pair $(H_0, V)$ with $N \ge 3$, a time $T^*$ such that for all
$T \ge T^*$ these objectives have no traps on $L^2([0,T])$? If not, which systems and objectives have traps?

## Why it matters

Gradient methods such as [[grape|GRAPE]] and [[krotov|Krotov]] are local. If the landscape has no traps, a local
search from almost any starting pulse reaches a global optimum; a trap with a large basin of attraction can stop it.
The answer explains why local optimal control works so often in practice, and tells when global methods are needed
(see [[control-landscapes]]).

## Known results

**The kinematic picture is trap-free.** As functions on the unitary group, objectives such as $J_O$ have only
global maxima, global minima and saddles [@rabitz2004landscape; @ho2006easy]. The dynamical landscape $J(f)$
inherits this structure at controls where the map $f \mapsto U_T$ is locally surjective (regular controls). Traps can
therefore only occur at singular controls, where this map loses rank [@wu2012singularities; @volkov2025stronger].

**Two-level systems are settled.** For $N = 2$ the absence of traps has been proved: first for the Landau–Zener
system [@pechen2012landauzener], then for general qubit control problems with long enough controls
[@pechen2014qubit], and then in the ultrafast regime — for state transfer between a wide range of initial and final
states [@pechen2017ultrafast] and for gate generation, including the phase-shift gates that earlier analyses had left
out [@volkov2021phaseshift; @volkov2023detailed].

**Higher-order traps for $N \ge 3$.** In a three-level $\Lambda$ system, the zero control is a critical point that is
not a global maximum and near which the Taylor expansion of the objective, up to some order, shows no direction of
increase. Such points slow down local searches and are called *higher-order traps* [@pechen2011traps;
@pechen2012lambda]. Their practical significance was disputed in a Comment and Reply [@rabitz2012comment;
@pechen2012reply]. Traps of arbitrarily high order exist in some strongly degenerate controllable systems
[@volkov2023higher]. In three-level systems with one forbidden transition, degenerate Bohr frequencies raise the
order (a seventh-order trap in a symmetric ladder system), and when the system is made uncontrollable the zero control
becomes a true trap [@volkov2025stronger]. A higher-order trap is not necessarily a local maximum; all known examples
of higher-order traps in controllable systems are constant controls [@volkov2025stronger].

**True traps at short times.** de Fouquieres and Schirmer found critical points of the $L^2$ landscape that are
traps, for state-transfer and gate problems [@defouquieres2013landscapes]. According to [@volkov2025stronger], these
examples have $N \ge 4$ and control times that are not very large, and the traps disappear at larger times.

**Generic systems.** Russell, Rabitz and Wu argued, with a geometric transversality argument, that the landscape is
trap-free for all Hamiltonians outside a set of measure zero [@russell2017trapfree]. A Comment pointed out errors in
the proof [@zhdanov2018comment], and the authors replied [@russell2018reply]; the generic statement is therefore
contested.

**The gate objective matters.** On $SU(N)$, the trace fidelity $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}(W^\dagger U)$ has
kinematic traps [@defouquieres2013landscapes]; for every $N \ge 5$ there are kinematic local extrema that are not
global [@birtea2022constraint]. These kinematic traps disappear when the objective ignores the global phase — an
optimization over the projective unitary group, as with $J_W$ above [@defouquieres2013landscapes].

## Status

**Partial progress.** The question is answered for $N = 2$: there are no traps. For $N \ge 3$, there is no proof
that controllable systems are trap-free at long times, and no true trap of such a system at long times is known; the
known obstructions are higher-order traps at constant controls. The proof for generic systems is disputed.
