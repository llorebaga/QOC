## Statement

Consider the finite-dimensional control system

$$\dot x = A x + \sum_{i=1}^{p} u_i(t)\, B_i x, \qquad x \in \mathbb C^n,$$

with skew-adjoint matrices $A, B_1, \dots, B_p$, unbounded controls $u_i \in L^\infty$, and the time unit fixed by
$\lVert A\rVert = 1$ (operator norm). The state moves on the unit sphere $S^{2n-1} \subset \mathbb C^n$. The
**minimum time** of the system is the worst case, over pairs of states, of the shortest time needed to connect them:

$$T(\Sigma) = \sup_{X, Y \in S^{2n-1}}\ \inf\big\{T \ge 0 : \text{a trajectory goes from } X \text{ to } Y
\text{ in time } T\big\}.$$

**Conjecture** (Agrachev). There is a universal $\delta > 0$ such that, whatever the dimension $n$ and the system,
either $T(\Sigma) = 0$ or $T(\Sigma) \ge \delta$ [@gauthier2021gap].

## Why it matters

$T(\Sigma)$ is the [[quantum-speed-limits|quantum speed limit]] of the whole control system: the time below which
some state-to-state task becomes impossible, however strong the controls. If the controls alone generate a group
that acts transitively on the sphere, every task can be done arbitrarily fast and $T(\Sigma) = 0$. Otherwise the drift
must be used, and the question is whether the minimal time can be arbitrarily small — for instance in large systems
— or is bounded below in units of the drift. Estimates of the controllability time had been obtained for particular
classes, for example single-input systems on compact Lie groups [@agrachev2006controllability].

## Known results

**Reduction to geometry.** Let $G \subset U(n)$ be the connected group generated by the control directions $B_i$,
and let

$$D(G) = \sup_{X, Y \in S^{2n-1}}\ \inf_{g, h \in G} \lVert gX - hY\rVert$$

be the largest distance between two of its orbits on the sphere. If $G$ is transitive, $D(G) = T(\Sigma) = 0$.
Otherwise, since the drift moves the state at speed at most $\lVert A\rVert = 1$ while the controls only move it
along orbits, Gauthier and Rossi show

$$\sin\!\Big(\frac{T(\Sigma)}{2}\Big) \;\ge\; \frac{D(G)}{2},$$

so a universal gap for the orbit-space diameter $D(G)$ gives one for $T(\Sigma)$ [@gauthier2021gap].

**All cases except spin representations.** Gauthier and Rossi proved that diameter gap for every unitary action except
the *basic spinor actions* (built from basic spin representations of spin groups), which proves the conjecture for
all other systems. If the dimension $n$ is neither a power of 2 nor in an explicit finite list of exceptions, they
obtain explicitly: either $T(\Sigma) = 0$ or $\sin\big(T(\Sigma)/2\big) \ge \tfrac12\sqrt{2 - \sqrt 2}$
[@gauthier2021gap].

**The diameter gap in general.** Gorodski, Lange, Lytchak and Mendes proved that for any group acting by isometries on
a unit sphere of dimension at least 2, the quotient space has diameter 0 or at least a universal constant
$\varepsilon > 0$, independent of the dimension. Unitary actions on $S^{2n-1}$ are isometric, so the spin cases are
covered; the authors refer to Gauthier and Rossi for the relevance of the problem to control theory
[@gorodski2022diameter].

## Status

**Solved.** Together, the inequality above [@gauthier2021gap] and the general diameter gap [@gorodski2022diameter]
give the conjecture for every finite-dimensional system. We have not found a paper that states this combination as a
separate theorem; it is our reading of the two papers and is marked for expert review. If you know a reference that
states it, please tell us.
