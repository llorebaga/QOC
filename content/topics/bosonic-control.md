**Bosonic control** steers quantum harmonic oscillators: microwave cavities, the motion of trapped ions, optical and
mechanical modes. An oscillator has infinitely many levels. A single long-lived mode can therefore hold an
error-correctable qubit — in cat, binomial or grid (GKP) codes — without the many physical qubits other codes need
[@gottesman2001gkp; @joshi2021bosonic]. The catch is that the oscillator's own evolution is linear. Drives that are
linear in the field only displace states, and quadratic Hamiltonians generate only Gaussian operations. Every
non-Gaussian state, from a Fock state to a cat state, needs a nonlinearity, and most bosonic control is about using a
weak one well.

## Control in infinite dimensions

- **Controllability.** For bilinear systems in infinite dimensions, exact controllability with bounded controls is
  ruled out in general [@ball1982bilinear], so the useful notion is *approximate* controllability. It has been proven
  for the Schrödinger equation with a discrete, non-resonant spectrum, using Galerkin (truncated) approximations
  [@chambrion2009controllability]. See [[controllability]].
- **Gaussian versus universal.** Linear optics and squeezing, generated by Hamiltonians at most quadratic in the
  field, make up Gaussian quantum information [@braunstein2005cv; @weedbrook2012gaussian]. Adding a single
  higher-order nonlinearity, such as a Kerr interaction, makes the set universal [@lloyd1999cv]. The Gaussian part
  is also the domain of [[linear-quantum-systems]].
- **Truncation.** Numerical optimization works in a space cut off at some photon number. A result is trustworthy only
  if it does not change when the cutoff is raised, and if the controlled states stay well away from the edge.

## Oscillators controlled through a qubit

Most experiments couple the oscillator to a nonlinear ancilla, such as a transmon or an ion's internal levels, and
control both.

- **Law–Eberly.** Any state of a field mode can be prepared by alternating qubit rotations and qubit–oscillator
  exchange [@law1996arbitrary]. In circuit QED this produced Fock states [@hofheinz2008fock] and arbitrary
  superpositions of them [@hofheinz2009synthesizing].
- **SNAP and displacements.** In the strong dispersive regime, the qubit frequency depends on the photon number, so
  qubit drives can give each Fock state its own phase. This is the selective number-dependent arbitrary phase (SNAP)
  gate [@heeres2015snap]. With displacements, SNAP gates give universal control of the oscillator and prepare
  $|n\rangle$ in $O(\sqrt n)$ steps rather than $O(n)$ [@krastanov2015universal]. Optimizing the gate parameters and
  then the pulse envelopes prepared cat, binomial, GKP and cubic-phase states, robustly against fluctuations of the
  system parameters [@kudra2022snap].
- **Optimal control of the full system.** With an accurate Hamiltonian, numerical optimal control of the
  coupled cavity–transmon system realized decoherence-limited operations (98.5% inferred fidelity) on a qubit encoded
  in four-component cat states [@heeres2017logical].
- **Weak coupling, amplified.** Echoed conditional displacements use strong drives to amplify a weak dispersive
  coupling. Operations run more than an order of magnitude faster than the bare coupling allows, preparing Fock
  states, squeezed vacuum and binomial and GKP logical states [@eickbusch2022ecd].
- **Gates that tolerate ancilla errors.** Because the ancilla decoheres much faster than the cavity, gates can be
  designed so that ancilla errors are corrected in real time rather than passed on to the logical qubit
  [@reinhold2020errorcorrected].

## Engineered nonlinearities and dissipation

- **Cat states.** Cat states of about 100 photons were encoded deterministically in a cavity [@vlastakis2013cat]. In
  a Kerr resonator with a two-photon drive, cat states become eigenstates, can be prepared robustly against
  single-photon loss, and can be initialized non-adiabatically with transitionless driving [@puri2017kerr]. This led
  to the Kerr-cat qubit [@grimm2020kerrcat]. See [[shortcuts-to-adiabaticity]].
- **Dissipative cats.** Engineered two-photon loss confines the field to the cat manifold [@leghtas2015twophoton;
  @mirrahimi2014cat]. See [[reservoir-engineering]].

## Grid states and error correction

GKP codes protect a qubit against small shifts in phase space [@gottesman2001gkp]. Grid states were prepared and
controlled in the motion of a trapped ion [@fluhmann2019gkp] and error-corrected with a feedback protocol in a
superconducting cavity [@campagneibarcq2020gkp]. A GKP qubit whose parameters were optimized with model-free
reinforcement learning stayed coherent 2.27 times longer than the best of its physical components
[@sivak2023beyond]. See
[[error-correction]], [[feedback-control]] and [[machine-learning-control]].

## Limitations

- Models must include the small terms that dominate at high photon number: self-Kerr, cross-Kerr, higher-order
  dispersive shifts and drive-induced frequency shifts.
- The ancilla is usually the noisiest element, so control speed is a trade-off between ancilla decoherence and the
  strength of the nonlinearity.
- Fidelities are hard to verify: Wigner or characteristic-function tomography of large states is slow and depends on
  the same imperfect ancilla.
